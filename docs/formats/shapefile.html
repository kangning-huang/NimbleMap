Formats · .shp

Open a Shapefile as a map in one drop

A Shapefile is a set of files that hold map shapes — boundaries, roads, storm tracks — plus a table of attributes. The main piece is the .shp; keep the matching .dbf, .shx and .prj beside it (or open the .zip they came in).

Download NimbleMap Shapefile viewer NimbleMap or QGIS?

How

Open it in NimbleMap

Drag the .shp or its .zip into NimbleMap, or double-click it in Finder. Attributes show in a table and on a card when you click a shape. Older files in Chinese or other encodings are read with their .cpg.

Why not Preview, a browser tab, or QGIS

When a simple map viewer is enough

Browser shapefile viewers ask you to upload the file, and Mac Quick Look apps only peek. A full map desk such as QGIS can open a Shapefile, but you usually need a project and a coordinate system first. NimbleMap is for when you just need to see the file, stack another layer on top, and export a map image — files stay on your computer.

Questions

Common questions

Is there free software to open a Shapefile on Mac or Windows?

Yes. NimbleMap is free. Double-click the .shp, or drag it or its .zip into the app. Keep the sidecar files next to it.

Can I open a Shapefile without QGIS?

Yes. Drag it into NimbleMap. No project to create and no coordinate system to set.

Why do my CSV points not sit on my Shapefile?

Often the Shapefile is projected (metres) while the CSV holds latitude and longitude. NimbleMap reads the .prj and converts so both line up.
